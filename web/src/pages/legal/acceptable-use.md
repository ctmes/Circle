<p><strong>This policy is a draft prepared for review.</strong> It has not been settled by a lawyer.</p>

## 1. Scope

This Acceptable Use Policy applies to everyone who uses Circle, and forms part of the [Terms of Service](/legal/terms). If you invite someone into a Circle, you are responsible for their compliance with it.

## 2. Do not break the law

You must not use the Service to do anything unlawful, or to store or transmit material that is unlawful. That includes material that infringes copyright, trade marks, patents, trade secrets or moral rights; material that breaches a confidentiality obligation or an export control; and material that is defamatory, harassing, or that sexualises a child.

You must not use the Service in a way that breaches a privacy law applicable to you, including uploading personal information you have no lawful basis to hold or to disclose to the other parties in a Circle.

## 3. Do not attack the Service

You must not:

- probe, scan or test the vulnerability of the Service except under the [responsible disclosure policy](/legal/responsible-disclosure);
- breach or circumvent authentication, rate limits, quotas, or the access controls that separate parties within a Circle;
- attempt to access another organisation's data, another party's party-scoped threads, or any account that is not yours;
- interfere with the Service or its infrastructure, including by denial of service or by deliberately overloading it;
- introduce malware, or upload a file you know or suspect is malicious;
- reverse engineer, decompile or disassemble the Service, except to the extent that restriction is prohibited by law; or
- crawl or scrape the Service, or use it through an automated client other than the documented API.

## 4. Do not corrupt the record

These rules exist because the product's value is that its record can be relied on later.

- **Do not misrepresent origin.** Do not upload evidence under an identity that is not yours, or configure the Service to attribute an action to someone who did not take it.
- **Do not share accounts.** A credential belongs to one person. A record that says two people used one login is worth nothing in a dispute.
- **Do not fabricate.** Do not deliberately file a claim, an approval or a commitment you know to be false, or cite a location in evidence that does not support the claim you attach it to.
- **Do not arrange around approval.** Do not configure, script or delegate approvals so that an action with a side effect proceeds without a human decision by someone holding the authority of the party that bears it.
- **Do not tamper.** Do not attempt to alter, delete or reorder audit events, or to modify an exported packet and present it as one the Service produced.

## 5. Rules for shared workspaces

A Circle is shared with organisations that are not yours. The following apply in addition to the Terms:

- Share evidence only where you have the right to share it with each party admitted to that Circle.
- Do not use the Service to obtain a counterparty's commercially sensitive information for a purpose outside the mission of that Circle.
- Do not re-disclose material from a Circle outside the Circle except as permitted by your own agreement with the other parties. The Service records access and can deny download; it cannot follow a file once you have lawfully exported it, and the obligation is yours.
- Do not admit an individual to a Circle who is not entitled, under your own arrangements, to see what that Circle holds.

## 6. Rules for agents

- Declare an agent's mandate honestly. Classify each tool by its actual consequence, not by what it is called.
- Do not point an agent at evidence you have not marked agent-readable, or attempt to widen its retrieval beyond what the access gate permits.
- Do not connect a credential you are not authorised to use, and do not connect a credential with broader scope than the agent's declared tools require.
- Do not use an agent to communicate externally on behalf of another party, or to represent an agent's draft output as a human decision.
- Do not use the Service to build a competing product, or to extract model outputs at volume for training or benchmarking, without our written consent.

## 7. Reporting a violation, and what we will do

Report abuse to <span class="todo">[legal@circle.example]</span>. Report a security vulnerability under the [responsible disclosure policy](/legal/responsible-disclosure) instead.

Where we reasonably believe this policy has been breached we may suspend a member, a Circle or an account. We will tell you what we have done and why, and we will restore access as soon as the cause is resolved. Where the breach is serious or repeated, we may terminate under clause 14 of the Terms.

Suspension is a blunt instrument, so we will use the narrowest one available: suspending one member rather than a Circle, and a Circle rather than an account, wherever that addresses the risk. Where a suspension would prevent a counterparty from meeting a deadline recorded in a Circle, we will tell the affected parties that access has been suspended — not why, which is between us and the account holder.
