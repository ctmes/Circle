## 2. The two roles we act in

This is the most important distinction in this policy, and everything below depends on it.

**As controller.** For information about the people who deal with us — account holders, members signing in, people who email us, visitors to the website — we decide why and how the information is handled. This policy governs that information.

**As processor.** For the content inside a Circle — uploaded evidence, claims, decisions, approvals, commitments, goals, comment threads, audit events — our customer decides why and how it is handled, and we act on their instructions. That includes any personal information that happens to be inside an uploaded document, a site photograph or a recorded voice note.

If you are a member of a Circle and want to know why your employer or a counterparty put something into it, or you want it changed or removed, ask that organisation. We will refer your request to them and support them in answering it, but we cannot make that decision for them. Our obligations as processor are set out in the [Data Processing Addendum](/legal/dpa).

**Sensitive information.** We do not seek sensitive information (health, racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious beliefs, sexual orientation, criminal record, biometric or genetic data) about our own users, and we ask you not to send it to us in support correspondence. Customer content is a different matter: a Circle can hold anything a customer puts into it, and if a customer intends to place sensitive information there, that customer is responsible for having a lawful basis and for the additional protections its own obligations require.

**Children.** The Service is not directed at anyone under 18, and we do not knowingly collect information about children. If you believe we have, contact the privacy officer and we will delete it.

**Anonymity.** Under APP 2 you may deal with us anonymously or under a pseudonym where that is lawful and practicable. It is practicable for a general website enquiry. It is not practicable for an account, because the entire purpose of the Service is attributing actions to identified people.

## 7. Security

We protect personal information with measures proportionate to the harm that would follow from its loss. Those measures are described in more detail in the [Security overview](/security), and include:

- encryption in transit (TLS) and at rest;
- passwords stored only as salted hashes, never recoverable;
- least-privilege access, with production access limited to named staff and logged;
- an append-only, hash-chained audit log, so alteration of the record is detectable;
- evidence served only through short-lived signed URLs, never a public bucket;
- separation of environments, and no production data in development or test.

No system is perfectly secure, and we do not claim otherwise.

**Data breaches.** If a data breach is likely to result in serious harm and we cannot prevent that harm, we will notify the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner and the affected individuals as quickly as the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme requires. Where the breach concerns customer content, we will notify the affected customer without undue delay so that they can meet their own obligations — including the GDPR's 72-hour deadline where it applies.

## 8. How long we keep it

The table in clause 3 gives the retention period for each category. Two general rules sit behind it:

- We destroy or de-identify personal information when it is no longer needed for any purpose for which it may be used or disclosed, and we are not required by law to keep it (APP 11.2).
- Deletion from production is followed by deletion from encrypted backups as those backups expire on their ordinary cycle, within <span class="todo">[60]</span> days.

The audit log is an exception worth naming: it is deliberately append-only, because a record that can be quietly edited is not a record. Audit entries are not individually deleted. They are removed with the Circle they belong to, on the schedule in the Terms.

## 10. If the GDPR or the UK GDPR applies to you

Where we act as controller of personal data of people in the EEA or the UK, we rely on the following legal bases:

- **Contract** (Art. 6(1)(b)) — to provide the Service to the organisation you belong to, and to authenticate you.
- **Legal obligation** (Art. 6(1)(c)) — tax, accounting and record-keeping.
- **Legitimate interests** (Art. 6(1)(f)) — securing the Service, preventing misuse, keeping an attributable record, and responding to business enquiries. We have balanced these against your interests and consider them proportionate; you may object at any time.
- **Consent** (Art. 6(1)(a)) — for non-essential cookies and for marketing email, which you may withdraw at any time without affecting what came before.

In addition to clause 9, you have the right to erasure, to restrict processing, to data portability in a structured machine-readable format, to object to processing based on legitimate interests, and to withdraw consent. We do not carry out automated decision-making that produces legal or similarly significant effects on you within the meaning of Art. 22 — agents draft; people decide.

You may complain to your local supervisory authority, or to the UK Information Commissioner's Office. <span class="todo">[If we offer the Service to EEA or UK individuals without an establishment there, an Art. 27 representative must be appointed and named here.]</span>

Where we act as processor, the [Data Processing Addendum](/legal/dpa) governs, and your route is through the customer who controls the data.
